What does it feel like to finish Yoga For Life?

Maybe you've been practising yoga for a while. And maybe something has started to shift. You no longer see it just as a series of postures. You're interested in its questions, its philosophy. What happens when you breathe, when you observe your mind, when an emotion appears, when you have a conflict, when you want something, when you don't know what you want, when you catch yourself reacting exactly the way you didn't want to react.

And then a question appears: how can I use all of this in my life?

That's what we're going to investigate during YFL.

We'll teach you to use yoga to
💥
Better understand what's happening inside you.

You'll learn to observe thoughts, emotions, sensations, impulses and patterns without having to react automatically to everything that arises.

💥
Investigate what you really want.

Because not everything we desire necessarily comes from us. Some things we've learned, inherited, or been conditioned into.

💥
Observe how you build your reality through language.

What you tell yourself, how you name what happens, which interpretations you turn into facts, and which stories you build from them.

💥
Relate differently to yourself and to others.

We'll use the yoga tradition to investigate boundaries, approval, comparison, listening, responsibility, and our own relational patterns.

💥
Understand what you do with conflict.

Not to learn to avoid it, but to use it as a privileged place from which to observe how you function.

💥
Understand what energy you're building your life with.

What nourishes you, what drains you, where you put your attention, what you're feeding, and what you're leaving without energy.

💥
Develop your own judgement.

YFL's goal isn't for you to end up thinking like Nati and Naylín, or like Patañjali. It's for you to become capable of observing, questioning, experimenting, and discovering for yourself.

💥
Build a deeper relationship with yoga.

Not just knowing more about yoga, but starting to recognise it in your own life.

What to expect

Yoga For Life is not a course you consume and finish.

It's not a collection of classes. It's not a training to accumulate postures. It's not a philosophy library. It's not therapy. And it's not an experience where two teachers tell you how you should live.

It's an 8-month applied training.

During these eight months we'll work simultaneously on your understanding of yoga, your practice, your capacity for observation, and your own life experience. We want there to be a permanent connection between what we study, what we practise, and what happens when you leave the room.

YFL's philosophy

Our method follows this path.

Tradition→ Question→ Experience→ Observation→ Discovery→ Integration→ Liberation

The yoga tradition offers us maps. But a map isn't much use if you never go out and walk. We don't want to give you closed answers. We want to teach you a way of investigating.

How it works

Once a month, 12 hours together.

Over 8 months you'll have a continuous experience. Once a month we'll meet for 12 hours to go deep into one of YFL's big questions. That's 96 hours of in-person training.

📍 The in-person sessions take place at Nati's studio in Jávea: Calle Barcelona 15.

First session · 10 October 2026

Each session integrates yoga philosophy, applied anatomy, asana, pranayama, meditation, contemplation, inquiry, conversation, and application to real situations. We don't study each discipline separately: we use all of them to investigate a single question.

And between sessions, the inquiry continues.

You'll have 4 regular VCY classes each month: you can practise with us every week, two classes with Nati and two with Naylín. These are our normal VCY classes, not an artificial class created to teach you YFL theory. You can observe the practice, ask yourself questions, and then come and ask us why we chose that sequence, that posture, that breath.

Every week, an online session.

30 minutes of questions and answers. A space to bring whatever is happening in your practice and your life, because the important question doesn't always show up while you're listening to an explanation. Sometimes it shows up on a Tuesday, in a conversation, in a thought that keeps returning.

Your part of the process

YFL doesn't work if we do all the work.

🧘‍♀️ Daily personal asana practice

🧘 Daily meditation

📓 Observation journal

📚 Selected readings

🔎 Life-application experiments

The intention isn't to load you with homework. It's for what we investigate to have a real chance to show up in your experience.

YFL's 8 gates

A journey, not a list of topics.

Module 1 · 9 October 2027

What do I really want?

Investigating desire, intention, and conditioning, to distinguish between what I want, what I believe I should want, and what I'm actually choosing.

Module 2

What's in my mind?

Investigating thoughts, attention, identification, judgements, and interpretations, to start distinguishing between what happens and what my mind tells me about what happens.

Module 3

What words am I using, and what am I creating with them?

Investigating the relationship between thought, word, and action, to observe how the way we name reality shapes the experience we build.

Module 4

What do I feel, and what do I do with what I feel?

Investigating sensations, emotions, impulses, resistance, and reaction, to discover that feeling something doesn't necessarily mean having to act from it.

Module 5

How do I relate to myself and to others?

Investigating the Yamas and Niyamas through our real relationships, to start recognising our own patterns where before we only saw other people's.

Module 6

What do I do with conflict?

What happened, what I interpreted, what I felt, what I did, what my action produced. To turn conflict into a place of observation.

Module 7

What energy am I living with?

Investigating Prana, the Gunas, and our relationship with effort, rest, attention, and energy, to observe what nourishes me, what drains me, and where I'm putting my energy.

Module 8

What did I do in what I did? Who am I?

We integrate the whole journey: what we wanted, what we thought, what we said, what we felt, how we related, how we responded to conflict, how we used our energy.

We don't want you to end up with more answers

We want you to end up with better questions.

Imagine that after YFL: you have a conflict and can observe what's happening before reacting. You have a decision to make and can tell desire apart from conditioning. You feel an emotion and can experience it without automatically turning it into an action. You catch yourself thinking something and can ask whether you're observing a fact or an interpretation. You relate to someone and can recognise which of your own patterns is showing up. You're tired and can start asking yourself what you're feeding and what you're exhausting.

That's bringing yoga into your life.

YFL doesn't want to create dependency

We don't want you to ask us "what do I do?"

We want you to be able to ask yourself: what am I observing? What does the tradition say about this? What am I really experiencing? What am I interpreting? What can I discover here? And to be able to keep investigating.

That's why we talk about liberation. Not as a promise that you'll become a person who never suffers again, but as a process of gradually letting go of being fully driven by your automatic patterns, and developing more and more capacity to observe and choose.

Who's behind YFL?

Nati and Naylín.

YFL was born from our own practice, study, and experience. We've spent years teaching yoga and exploring what happens when practice stops staying only on the mat, and we've reached a conclusion: yoga has so much more to offer than learning to do a posture correctly.

That's why we built YFL. Not to create another training that teaches more content, but to create a space where we can investigate what happens when we use yoga as a tool to look at our own lives.

Is it for you?

Two lists, so you can decide with clarity.

YFL might be for you if

✔️ You already have a relationship with yoga.
✔️ You want to go beyond the postures.
✔️ You're interested in yoga philosophy, but want to experience it.
✔️ You have questions about yourself and how you live.
✔️ You want to understand your patterns.
✔️ You want to develop your own judgement.
✔️ You're willing to practise and observe between sessions.
✔️ You want to discover what yoga can do for your life.

Probably not, if

❌ You're only looking to improve your asanas.
❌ You want a quick certification.
❌ You want someone to tell you how to live.
❌ You don't want to dedicate time to your own practice.
❌ You'd rather accumulate theory than experience.
❌ You're looking for closed answers.

And that's okay. We don't want to be for everyone. We want to bring together the people who genuinely want to do this inquiry.

Still have doubts?
"I don't know enough yoga."
You don't need to know it all. But you do need enough prior experience to experience the practices from the inside.
"What if I don't agree with you two?"
We don't need you to agree. We want you to develop your own judgement.
"What if this is too theoretical?"
Theory will never be the destination. We'll use it to come back to experience.
"What if I want to apply all of this to my life?"
That's exactly what YFL is for.
"What if I've already been practising for years?"
Then maybe it's time to ask yourself: what else can I do with everything I've already learned?

You can keep asking yourself the same questions. Or you can start investigating them.

For eight months you can stay exactly where you are, or you can step into an experience designed to bring yoga from the mat into what you're really living. We don't know what you'll discover, and we don't want to tell you either. Because that's precisely the inquiry.
